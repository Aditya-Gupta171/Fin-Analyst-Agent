# Basis for offer price — valuation against peers and past transactions

## What it measures
The "Basis for Offer Price" section justifies the price band. It presents quantitative factors — basic and diluted
EPS, P/E at the floor and cap price, return on net worth, net asset value per share — a comparison with listed
peers, and key performance indicators (KPIs). SEBI also requires disclosure of the weighted average cost of
acquisition (WACA) of shares through recent primary issuances and secondary transactions, compared with the floor
and cap price.

## How to read it
- Compare the P/E at the upper end of the band with the median of the listed peers the company itself chose. A
  large premium needs clearly superior growth, returns or margins.
- Check whether the peers are genuinely comparable in scale, business mix and geography; a company can flatter its
  valuation by choosing expensive peers.
- Use diluted EPS of the latest full restated year, and adjust for exceptional items.
- The WACA disclosure shows at what prices shares changed hands before the IPO. A price band many times the price
  paid in transactions within the previous eighteen months deserves explanation.
- The cap of the price band must not exceed 120% of the floor.
- With negative EPS, P/E is not meaningful; valuation rests on KPIs such as revenue growth, contribution margin or
  gross order value, which must be defined, audited or certified, and disclosed for the restated periods.

## Red flags
- P/E premium above about 50% to the peer median without a growth or return advantage.
- Peer set changed between the DRHP and the RHP to more expensive companies.
- Recent secondary transactions or pre-IPO placements at prices far below the band.
- KPIs presented selectively or defined differently from peers.

## Benign explanations
- Market leadership or growth materially above peers, visible in the restated numbers.
- Few or no truly comparable listed peers in India, with global comparisons provided.

## Where to find it in Indian filings
- DRHP / RHP: "Basis for Offer Price", "Capital Structure" (history of allotments and transfers) and the price band
  advertisement published before the offer opens.

## Questions for management
- Why were these peers selected, and how does the company compare on growth and returns?
- At what prices did shares change hands in the eighteen months before the offer?
