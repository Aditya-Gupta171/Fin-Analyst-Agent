# Outstanding litigation and material developments

## What it measures
The "Outstanding Litigation and Material Developments" section lists proceedings involving the company, its
subsidiaries, promoters and directors: criminal proceedings, actions by statutory or regulatory authorities,
disciplinary actions by SEBI or stock exchanges against promoters in the last five years, tax proceedings, and
other civil litigation above a materiality threshold set by the board. It also covers outstanding dues to creditors,
including micro and small enterprises.

## How to read it
- Scale the aggregate amount involved against net worth and profit.
- Criminal proceedings against promoters and directors, even if early-stage, are a governance and key-person risk.
- Regulatory actions (SEBI, RBI, tax authorities, environmental regulators) can affect licences and operations.
- Tax proceedings are frequent; judge by amount, forum and history.
- Read the materiality policy itself: a very high threshold can keep significant cases out of the list.

## Red flags
- Aggregate litigation against the company above about a tenth of net worth.
- Criminal proceedings or regulatory orders against promoters or directors, including in other group companies.
- Actions relating to fraud, money laundering or defaults by group companies.
- Wilful defaulter or fraudulent borrower classification of the company, promoters or directors, which ICDR bars
  from accessing the market.

## Benign explanations
- Routine consumer or employment cases with small amounts.
- Proceedings initiated by the company to recover dues.

## Where to find it in Indian filings
- DRHP / RHP: "Outstanding Litigation and Material Developments", "Risk Factors", and "Government and Other
  Approvals".

## Questions for management
- What is the status and expected timeline of the most significant proceedings?
- Could any proceeding affect a licence, a material contract or a promoter's role?
