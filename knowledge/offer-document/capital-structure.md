# Capital structure — promoter holding, acquisition cost and lock-in

## What it measures
The "Capital Structure" section shows share capital history, pre- and post-offer shareholding, the average cost at
which promoters and selling shareholders acquired their shares, lock-in details and any pre-IPO placements.

## How to read it
- **Skin in the game:** post-offer promoter holding shows alignment with minority shareholders. Minimum promoter
  contribution of 20% of post-offer capital is locked in, generally for eighteen months (three years where the
  objects include capital expenditure); promoter holding above the minimum is locked in for a shorter period.
- **Acquisition cost:** the ratio of the upper price band to promoters' average cost shows the gain being realised by
  selling promoters. Very large multiples are common for founders but should be weighed against what IPO investors
  pay.
- **Recent allotments:** shares issued within the year before the offer at prices below the band, especially to
  promoters or related parties, dilute IPO investors' value.
- **Anchor investors:** half of anchor shares are locked in for 30 days and the remainder for 90 days; a large
  anchor book reduces immediate post-listing supply but not later supply.
- Convertible instruments and ESOP pools affect post-offer dilution.

## Red flags
- Post-offer promoter holding below about 35% in a promoter-run company.
- Bonus issues or preferential allotments to promoters shortly before the offer.
- Selling shareholders who acquired shares within the past year at prices far below the band.
- Large ESOP grants just before listing at nominal exercise prices.

## Benign explanations
- Professionally managed, investor-backed companies without a traditional promoter, where low promoter holding is
  structural.
- Founders' low acquisition cost reflecting incorporation-stage capital.

## Where to find it in Indian filings
- DRHP / RHP: "Capital Structure", "Our Promoters and Promoter Group", "The Offer" and "Basis for Offer Price" (WACA
  disclosures).

## Questions for management
- What will promoter holding be after the offer, and what are the lock-in periods?
- Were any shares allotted or transferred in the year before the offer, at what prices and to whom?
