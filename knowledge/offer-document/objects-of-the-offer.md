# Objects of the offer — fresh issue, offer for sale and use of proceeds

## What it measures
An initial public offer can have two parts. The **fresh issue** creates new shares and the money goes to the
company. The **offer for sale (OFS)** sells existing shares and the money goes to the selling shareholders —
promoters, private equity or early investors. The "Objects of the Offer" section explains how the company will use
fresh issue proceeds, typically capex, working capital, debt repayment, acquisitions and general corporate
purposes (GCP).

## How to read it
- The OFS share of the offer shows whether the IPO funds the company or provides an exit. An OFS-dominated offer is
  not bad in itself, but the valuation must stand on the business as it is today, since no new capital enters it.
- Under ICDR Regulation 7(2), GCP may not exceed 25% of the gross proceeds of the fresh issue. Amounts for
  unidentified acquisitions or investments are also capped, and together with GCP may not exceed 35%.
- Specific objects must be supported: capex by project cost estimates and quotations, working capital by a
  working-capital assessment, debt repayment by the list of loans to be repaid.
- For fresh issues above ₹100 crore, a monitoring agency (a credit rating agency) must report on the use of
  proceeds; deviation from stated objects requires shareholder approval.
- Debt repayment objects reduce finance costs and risk but do not add capacity; check whether the loans being repaid
  were taken shortly before the offer and for what purpose.

## Red flags
- GCP close to or above the 25% limit, or large amounts for unidentified acquisitions.
- OFS above about half of the offer by shareholders who acquired shares recently at far lower prices.
- Proceeds to repay loans taken from promoters or group companies, or to acquire assets from related parties.
- Vague working-capital objects without an assessment of requirements.
- Deployment schedules extending several years without firm commitments.

## Benign explanations
- A private equity exit in a mature, cash-generative company that does not need capital.
- Debt repayment that materially lowers leverage in a capital-intensive business with a clear growth plan.

## Where to find it in Indian filings
- DRHP / RHP sections: "The Offer", "Objects of the Offer" (including schedule of deployment and means of finance),
  and "Offer Document Summary".
- After listing: monitoring agency reports and statements of deviation or variation filed with stock exchanges
  under LODR.

## Questions for management
- Who are the selling shareholders, and at what average cost did they acquire their shares?
- What exactly will general corporate purposes fund?
- For debt repayment objects: when and why were the loans taken?
