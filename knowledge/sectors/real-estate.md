# Sector primer — real estate developers

## What it measures
Residential developers sell apartments before and during construction. In India, most residential projects recognise
revenue at a point in time, on completion or handover, under Ind AS 115, so reported revenue lags sales by years.
Key measures:
- Pre-sales (bookings) value and area sold, and collections.
- Inventory: land, construction work in progress and unsold completed units.
- Customer advances (contract liabilities), which fund construction.
- Net debt and interest capitalised into inventory.
- Land bank and joint development agreements with landowners.

## How to read it
- Judge operating momentum by pre-sales and collections, not by reported revenue or profit.
- Under the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, a large share of customer collections for a project
  (generally 70%) must go into a separate account used only for that project's construction and land cost, which
  limits diversion of funds across projects.
- Borrowing costs are capitalised into inventory under Ind AS 23; reported finance costs understate the interest
  burden.
- Joint development agreements reduce land cost but share revenue with landowners.

## Red flags
- Collections well below pre-sales, suggesting cancellations or buyer financing stress.
- Unsold completed inventory growing.
- Rising debt despite strong reported pre-sales.
- Large advances paid to related parties for land.
- Projects with RERA registration lapses or delays.

## Benign explanations
- Lumpy reported revenue as large projects are handed over in particular years.

## Where to find it in Indian filings
- Investor presentations (pre-sales, collections, launches), inventories and contract liabilities notes, borrowing
  cost capitalisation, project-wise RERA disclosures on state regulator websites.

## Questions for management
- What were collections versus pre-sales and the cancellation rate?
- How much interest was capitalised into inventory?
