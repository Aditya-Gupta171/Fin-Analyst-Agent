# Sector primer — FMCG and consumer brands

## What it measures
Consumer staples and discretionary brands sell through distributors, modern trade, e-commerce and quick commerce.
Key measures:
- Underlying volume growth versus price-led growth; rural versus urban demand.
- Gross margin against key commodity inputs (palm oil, crude derivatives for packaging, milk, wheat, sugar).
- Advertising and promotion spend as a share of revenue.
- Working capital, typically low or negative because distributors pay quickly and suppliers are paid later.
- Royalty or technical fees paid to foreign parent companies by listed Indian subsidiaries of multinationals.

## How to read it
- Price-led growth during inflation can mask falling volumes.
- Gross margin expansion from falling commodity prices is often partly reinvested in advertising.
- Revenue is net of trade discounts and schemes under Ind AS 115; changes in trade schemes move revenue and margins.
- Negative working capital means growth generates cash, and a shift towards positive working capital is a warning.

## Red flags
- Revenue spikes at quarter end with higher receivables or distributor inventory (channel stuffing).
- Royalty payments to a parent rising as a share of revenue; these are related party transactions requiring approval
  under LODR Regulation 23 when material.
- Advertising cuts supporting margins while volumes fall.
- Working capital turning positive as the company pushes stock to distributors.

## Benign explanations
- Festive season timing shifting sales between quarters.
- Planned stocking ahead of a price increase announced in advance.

## Where to find it in Indian filings
- Investor presentations and results commentary (volume growth), advertisement expense within other expenses,
  related party note for royalties, receivables and inventory notes.

## Questions for management
- What was underlying volume growth, and how much of growth came from price?
- How did distributor inventory levels change during the quarter?
