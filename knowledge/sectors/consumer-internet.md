# Sector primer — consumer internet and new-age companies

## What it measures
Marketplaces, food and quick commerce, fintech and software-as-a-service platforms often list before reaching
profitability, typically under ICDR Regulation 6(2). Key measures:
- Gross order value (GOV) or gross merchandise value, take rate, and revenue.
- Contribution margin after delivery, payment and discount costs.
- Adjusted EBITDA (usually excluding ESOP costs) versus reported EBITDA.
- Customer acquisition cost, retention cohorts and lifetime value.
- Cash burn and runway.

## How to read it
- Whether the company is principal or agent under Ind AS 115 decides if revenue is gross order value or only
  commission; changes in business model shift reported revenue without changing economics.
- ESOP charges under Ind AS 102 are real costs paid in dilution; adjusted EBITDA that excludes them overstates
  profitability.
- Improving contribution margin with falling discounts is the most credible path to profit.
- KPIs in offer documents must be defined consistently and certified; compare definitions with listed peers.

## Red flags
- Revenue growth driven by discounts or incentives recorded as marketing expense rather than a reduction of revenue.
- Adjusted EBITDA positive only after excluding large recurring costs.
- Cash runway shorter than the stated path to profitability.
- Pre-IPO transactions at valuations far below the price band.

## Benign explanations
- Planned investment in new categories or cities with cohort data showing maturing unit economics.

## Where to find it in Indian filings
- Offer documents: "Basis for Offer Price" KPIs, "Our Business", risk factors on losses and negative cash flows;
  shareholder letters and quarterly results after listing.

## Questions for management
- What are contribution margins by cohort or city, and how have they changed?
- How long will current cash last at the present burn rate?
