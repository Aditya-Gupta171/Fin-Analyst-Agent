# Sector primer — manufacturing, chemicals, auto components and metals

## What it measures
Industrial companies convert raw materials into products using plants with high fixed costs. Key measures:
- Capacity, utilisation and volume growth.
- Gross margin and EBITDA per unit relative to raw material prices (steel, aluminium, crude derivatives, key
  chemical intermediates).
- Working capital days, particularly inventory and receivables.
- Capex relative to depreciation, CWIP and return on capital employed through the cycle.

## How to read it
- Margins track the lag between input cost changes and price revisions; expect temporary compression when costs rise
  and expansion when they fall.
- Utilisation drives operating leverage: new capacity depresses returns until it fills.
- Government incentives (production-linked incentive schemes, export incentives) are recognised as grants under
  Ind AS 20, often in other operating revenue; separate them when judging underlying margins and check whether
  eligibility conditions are met.
- Auto component and contract chemical makers often depend on a few customers; see customer concentration.

## Red flags
- Capacity expansion ahead of demand, with falling utilisation and rising debt.
- Inventory building faster than sales (production for stock absorbs fixed costs and flatters margin).
- Incentive income forming a large share of profit.
- Environmental or regulatory actions on plants, disclosed in litigation or approvals sections.

## Benign explanations
- Commodity-driven margin swings that reverse through the cycle and track peers.

## Where to find it in Indian filings
- MD&A and investor presentations (capacity, utilisation), inventories and PPE notes, government grants note,
  segment reporting.

## Questions for management
- What is current utilisation and the ramp-up plan for new capacity?
- How quickly are raw material cost changes passed on to customers?
