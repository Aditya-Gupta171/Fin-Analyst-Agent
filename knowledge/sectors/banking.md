# Sector primer — banks

## What it measures
A bank's raw material is money: it borrows through deposits and lends or invests. Working-capital days, debt to
equity and interest coverage are meaningless. Indian banks still prepare financial statements under Indian GAAP in
the format of the Third Schedule to the Banking Regulation Act, not under Ind AS and Schedule III. The measures that
matter:
- **Asset quality:** gross and net non-performing assets (a loan is non-performing when interest or principal is
  overdue for more than 90 days), provision coverage ratio, slippage ratio, restructured book, special mention
  accounts (SMA-1, SMA-2) as early stress.
- **Capital:** capital to risk-weighted assets ratio (CRAR) and common equity tier 1; RBI's minimum for Indian banks
  is 9% plus a 2.5% capital conservation buffer.
- **Funding:** CASA (current and savings account) share of deposits, deposit growth versus loan growth,
  credit-deposit ratio.
- **Profitability:** net interest margin, cost to income, credit cost, return on assets.

## How to read it
- Loan growth well above deposit growth strains funding and margins.
- Falling gross NPAs can come from write-offs rather than recoveries; check write-offs and upgrades.
- Treasury gains can flatter profit when bond yields fall.
- RBI's annual risk-based supervision can require additional provisions; divergence between bank-reported and
  RBI-assessed NPAs must be disclosed when material.

## Red flags
- Rising SMA-2 book or restructured loans ahead of NPA recognition.
- Provision coverage falling while gross NPAs rise.
- Concentrated exposure to a single group or stressed sector.
- CRAR near regulatory minimums, or placement under RBI's prompt corrective action framework.
- Material NPA divergence disclosures.

## Benign explanations
- NPA ratios rising from a very low base in a growing unsecured retail book with pricing that covers expected loss.

## Where to find it in Indian filings
- Quarterly results with asset quality and capital adequacy disclosures, Basel III Pillar 3 disclosures, annual
  report notes on NPA movement, provisions and divergence.

## Questions for management
- What are slippages, recoveries, upgrades and write-offs this period?
- What is the SMA-1 and SMA-2 book and the restructured portfolio?
