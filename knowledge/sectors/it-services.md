# Sector primer — IT services

## What it measures
Indian IT services companies earn most revenue in US dollars, euros and pounds from technology and outsourcing
services. Employee cost is the dominant expense. Key measures:
- Revenue growth in constant currency, by vertical, geography and service line.
- Utilisation, attrition, headcount additions and subcontracting cost.
- Deal wins (total contract value), book-to-bill and client concentration.
- EBIT margin, days sales outstanding including unbilled revenue, and cash conversion.

## How to read it
- Rupee depreciation lifts reported revenue and margins without any operational improvement; separate currency from
  underlying growth.
- Hedging gains and losses appear in revenue or other income depending on hedge accounting; check the cash flow
  hedge reserve in other comprehensive income.
- Fixed-price contracts use percentage-of-completion accounting, which puts revenue into unbilled balances and relies
  on cost estimates.
- Employee cost growing faster than revenue usually means falling utilisation or wage increases not passed on.
- Tax benefits from SEZ units are running off because new units could not claim them after March 2020, so effective
  tax rates are drifting up.

## Red flags
- DSO including unbilled revenue rising above peers.
- Growth concentrated in a few large clients or in a single deal.
- Rising subcontracting costs suggesting difficulty hiring.
- Large deal wins with front-loaded transition costs capitalised as contract assets.

## Benign explanations
- Temporary margin dips in quarters with annual wage hikes or visa costs.

## Where to find it in Indian filings
- Quarterly investor fact sheets and presentations (constant currency growth, utilisation, attrition, client
  metrics), revenue and contract balance notes, segment reporting.

## Questions for management
- What was constant currency growth and how much came from the largest clients?
- What share of revenue is fixed-price and how are cost estimates reviewed?
