# Statement reconciliation — can the numbers be trusted?

## What it measures
Financial statements are internally linked. Before drawing conclusions, an analyst confirms that the numbers being
used hold together:
- Total assets equal total equity and liabilities.
- Total equity equals share capital plus other equity (plus non-controlling interests in consolidated statements).
- Total income equals revenue from operations plus other income; PBT less tax equals profit for the period.
- Operating, investing and financing cash flows add up to the net change in cash, and opening cash plus that
  change equals closing cash.
- Closing cash in the cash flow statement agrees with the balance sheet, after known reconciling items.
- In quarterly results, the four quarters add up to the full year.

## How to read it
- A reconciliation failure is first a data question: was a line mis-read, a sub-total captured as a line item, a
  sign reversed, or a column from a different period picked up?
- Only after ruling out extraction problems is it a question about the filing itself.
- Tolerance matters: figures are rounded (usually to lakhs or crores), so small differences are expected; the
  engine tolerates a fraction of a percent.
- Failures cascade. If the balance sheet does not balance, every leverage, liquidity and return ratio built on it
  is unreliable, and findings based on those ratios should be held back.

## Red flags
- Differences that persist after re-checking the source table.
- Standalone and consolidated figures mixed within one dataset.
- Restated figures in an offer document that differ materially from the audited figures published earlier for the
  same year, without an explanation in the restatement adjustments note.

## Benign explanations
- Cash flow statements that net bank overdrafts repayable on demand against cash and cash equivalents, which the
  balance sheet shows separately as borrowings.
- Exchange differences on translating foreign operations' cash balances.
- Cash acquired or disposed of with subsidiaries.
- Profit from discontinued operations or share of associates presented after tax, which breaks a naive PBT-to-PAT
  bridge.
- Reclassification of prior-year figures, which filings disclose as "regrouped / reclassified wherever necessary".

## Where to find it in Indian filings
- The face of the balance sheet, statement of profit and loss and cash flow statement in Schedule III format.
- Cash and cash equivalents note reconciling the cash flow statement to the balance sheet.
- In offer documents, the "Restated Financial Information" including the statement of adjustments to audited
  financial statements.

## Questions for management
- Why do closing cash balances differ between the cash flow statement and the balance sheet?
- What adjustments were made in restating the financial statements, and why?
