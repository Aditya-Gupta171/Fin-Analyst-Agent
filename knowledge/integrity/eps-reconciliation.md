# EPS reconciliation — share count changes and dilution

## What it measures
Earnings per share under Ind AS 33 is profit attributable to equity holders divided by the weighted average number
of shares outstanding. Diluted EPS also assumes conversion of dilutive instruments such as employee stock options,
warrants and convertible securities. A rough check divides profit by shares outstanding at period end (share
capital divided by face value); a growing gap between profit growth and EPS growth reveals changes in share count.

## How to read it
- EPS growth below profit growth means shareholders were diluted: a fresh issue, a qualified institutions
  placement, ESOP allotments, conversion of warrants or preference shares.
- EPS growth above profit growth usually means a buyback.
- Bonus issues and share splits are applied retrospectively to all periods presented, so they should not create a
  divergence between periods; if they appear to, the comparative periods may not have been restated.
- In consolidated statements, use profit attributable to owners of the parent, not total profit including
  non-controlling interests.

## Red flags
- A persistent gap between basic and diluted EPS, indicating a large overhang of options or convertible
  instruments.
- Preferential allotments or warrants issued to promoters at prices well below market.
- Reported EPS inconsistent with profit and share capital by more than about 10% without a disclosed change in share
  count.

## Benign explanations
- An equity raise during the year, where weighted average shares are lower than year-end shares.
- A bonus issue or split after the balance sheet date that has been applied to EPS but not yet to share capital.
- Large non-controlling interests in consolidated profit.

## Where to find it in Indian filings
- EPS note in the financial statements and the EPS lines in quarterly results.
- Share capital note and the statement of changes in equity for issues, buybacks, bonus issues and splits.
- In offer documents, the "Capital Structure" section's history of equity share capital, and the "Basis for Offer
  Price" section, which uses diluted EPS.

## Questions for management
- How many shares were issued or bought back during the period, and at what prices?
- What is the outstanding pool of options, warrants and convertible instruments?
