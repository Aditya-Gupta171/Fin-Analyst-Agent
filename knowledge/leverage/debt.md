# Borrowings, leverage and debt capacity

## What it measures
Leverage compares borrowings with the equity base (debt to equity) and with the earnings available to service them
(net debt to EBITDA). Net debt subtracts cash, bank balances and liquid current investments from total borrowings.
Borrowings include current maturities of long-term debt, which Schedule III presents within current borrowings.

## How to read it
- Net debt to EBITDA answers "how many years of operating earnings would repay the debt?" Above about 3 to 3.5x
  lenders typically become restrictive; above 5x the company depends on refinancing.
- Debt to equity depends on business model. Capital-intensive infrastructure and utilities run higher leverage than
  asset-light services; lenders (banks, NBFCs) are judged on capital adequacy instead.
- Lease liabilities under Ind AS 116 are debt-like. Retailers, airlines and hospital chains can look lightly
  levered until leases are added.
- Look at maturity and cost: short-term borrowings funding long-term assets create refinancing risk; a rising
  average interest rate signals weaker credit standing.
- Promoter-level debt secured by company shares (see promoter pledge) and corporate guarantees given for group
  companies are hidden leverage.

## Red flags
- Net debt rising faster than EBITDA for several years.
- Borrowings growing by more than half in a year while free cash flow is negative.
- Negative EBITDA with positive net debt.
- Large corporate guarantees for related parties disclosed in contingent liabilities.
- Credit rating downgrades, rating agency "issuer not cooperating" status, or delays in servicing disclosed under
  SEBI's default disclosure requirements.
- Borrowings used to fund dividends or buybacks.

## Benign explanations
- A disclosed capex programme where assets are under construction and EBITDA will follow commissioning.
- Acquisition financing with a clear deleveraging plan, often from equity raise proceeds.
- Seasonal working capital borrowings repaid within the year.

## Where to find it in Indian filings
- Borrowings notes (non-current and current) with security, interest rate and repayment terms.
- Lease liabilities note (Ind AS 116) and the maturity analysis in the financial risk management note.
- Contingent liabilities note for guarantees; credit ratings in the annual report or offer document.
- In offer documents: "Financial Indebtedness" section and "Objects of the Offer" if proceeds repay debt.

## Questions for management
- What is the maturity profile of borrowings and the plan to refinance near-term maturities?
- What drove the increase in borrowings, and when will the funded assets generate cash?
- Are there guarantees or security provided for borrowings of group companies?
