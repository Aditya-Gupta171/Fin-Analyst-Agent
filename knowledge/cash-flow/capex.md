# Capital expenditure, CWIP and capitalisation of costs

## What it measures
Capital expenditure is cash spent on property, plant, equipment and intangible assets. Until an asset is ready for
use, its cost sits in capital work-in-progress (CWIP) and is not depreciated. Capex relative to depreciation shows
whether the company is expanding (well above 1x), maintaining (around 1x) or under-investing (below 1x).

## How to read it
- Link capex to the business plan: capacity additions should be followed by revenue growth once commissioned.
- CWIP that keeps growing relative to the operating asset base suggests delayed or stalled projects.
- Borrowing costs (Ind AS 23) and directly attributable costs are added to CWIP. During long projects this can move
  meaningful expense out of the income statement.
- Intangible assets under development (Ind AS 38) involve judgement about when research becomes development;
  capitalised development costs raise current profit.
- Sustained capex below depreciation can make free cash flow look strong while the asset base ages.

## Red flags
- CWIP above about half of net property, plant and equipment and still rising.
- CWIP ageing schedule showing projects in progress for more than three years, or projects "temporarily suspended".
- Capitalised development costs or pre-operative expenses growing faster than revenue.
- Capex paid as capital advances to related parties or to vendors with no visible progress.

## Benign explanations
- A disclosed, financed expansion with commissioning timelines and approvals on track.
- Large greenfield projects where multi-year construction is normal (cement, steel, power).

## Where to find it in Indian filings
- Property, plant and equipment and CWIP notes, including the CWIP ageing and completion schedule that Schedule III
  requires for projects overdue or over budget.
- Capital commitments in the notes; capital advances in other non-current assets.
- In offer documents: "Objects of the Offer" when proceeds fund capex, with project cost, schedule of deployment and
  the status of approvals; a monitoring agency reports on use of proceeds when the fresh issue exceeds ₹100 crore.

## Questions for management
- When are the projects in CWIP expected to be commissioned, and are any over budget?
- How much borrowing cost and other expense was capitalised this year?
- Are capital advances paid to related parties?
