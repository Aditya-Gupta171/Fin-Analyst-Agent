# Return on equity and return on assets

## What it measures
Return on equity (ROE) is profit after tax divided by average total equity; return on assets (ROA) uses average
total assets. The DuPont decomposition expresses ROE as net margin × asset turnover × financial leverage
(assets / equity), which shows where returns come from.

## How to read it
- High ROE driven by leverage is riskier than the same ROE driven by margin or turnover.
- A shrinking equity base — from losses, large dividends or buybacks — raises ROE mechanically.
- One-off gains in profit inflate ROE; use profit before exceptional items for trend analysis.
- For banks and NBFCs, ROA is the more comparable measure because leverage is inherent to the model; around 1% ROA
  is healthy for a bank.
- In quarterly data, annualise profit before comparing with annual figures.

## Red flags
- ROE rising only because equity is shrinking or leverage is increasing.
- ROE far above peers without a visible competitive advantage, combined with weak cash conversion.
- Negative equity (accumulated losses exceeding capital), which makes ROE meaningless.

## Benign explanations
- Asset-light business models (software, platforms, brands) that naturally earn high returns.
- Temporary dilution of ROE after an equity raise.

## Where to find it in Indian filings
- Schedule III additional regulatory information includes return on equity and net profit ratio.
- Offer documents disclose return on net worth in the "Basis for Offer Price" and "Summary of Financial
  Information" sections.

## Questions for management
- What returns do new investments earn compared with the existing business?
- What is the dividend and capital allocation policy?
