# Return on capital employed and asset turnover

## What it measures
Return on capital employed (ROCE) is operating EBIT divided by average capital employed (equity plus borrowings).
It measures the return generated on all capital invested, independent of the mix of debt and equity. Asset
turnover — revenue divided by average total assets — shows how intensively the asset base is used.

## How to read it
- Compare ROCE with the pre-tax cost of capital. For Indian companies, a sustained ROCE below roughly 12 to 14%
  generally means growth destroys value.
- ROCE can be decomposed into operating margin times capital turnover. Rising ROCE driven by turnover is usually
  more durable than a margin spike.
- Large cash balances depress ROCE; some analysts subtract surplus cash from capital employed for a cleaner view.
- Newly commissioned capacity depresses ROCE until utilisation builds; look at the trajectory.
- Revaluation of assets or large goodwill from acquisitions changes the denominator without changing the business.

## Red flags
- ROCE below the cost of capital for several years while the company keeps investing.
- High ROCE combined with weak cash conversion — the returns may not be real.
- Asset turnover declining steadily as capex rises, meaning new capacity is not generating proportionate revenue.

## Benign explanations
- A deliberate investment phase with capacity not yet commissioned (check CWIP).
- Recently raised equity temporarily held as cash.

## Where to find it in Indian filings
- Schedule III additional regulatory information requires return on capital employed and return on equity with
  explanations for changes above 25%.
- Segment reporting (Ind AS 108) for segment assets and results.
- Offer documents disclose key performance indicators, often including ROCE, in the "Basis for Offer Price".

## Questions for management
- What returns are expected on the current investment programme, and over what period?
- Which segments earn below the cost of capital, and what is the plan for them?
