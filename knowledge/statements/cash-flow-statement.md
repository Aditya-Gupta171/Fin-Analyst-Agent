# Reading the cash flow statement (Ind AS 7)

## What it measures
The statement of cash flows explains the change in cash and cash equivalents through operating, investing and
financing activities. Most Indian companies use the indirect method: profit before tax adjusted for non-cash items
(depreciation, unrealised foreign exchange, interest), then for working-capital changes, less taxes paid.

## How to read it
- The working-capital adjustments show exactly which balances absorbed or released cash.
- Classification choices under Ind AS 7 matter: non-financial companies may classify interest paid as operating or
  financing, and interest and dividends received as operating or investing. Compare like with like.
- Purchases of property, plant and equipment, investments in subsidiaries, loans given to related parties and
  movement in bank deposits sit in investing activities; large "loans given" deserve a look.
- Financing activities show borrowing, repayment, lease payments, dividends and equity raised.
- Cash and cash equivalents can be net of bank overdrafts repayable on demand.

## Red flags
- Operating cash flow boosted by non-recurring items (large advances from customers, sale of receivables).
- Cash repeatedly moved into bank deposits or mutual funds that are then pledged.
- Loans to related parties in investing activities.
- Dividends funded by new borrowing.

## Benign explanations
- Classification differences between peers that disappear on a consistent basis.

## Where to find it in Indian filings
- Annual report and restated financial information; the half-yearly cash flow statement filed with September
  quarter results.

## Questions for management
- Which working-capital items drove operating cash flow this year?
- What were loans and deposits placed during the year, and with whom?
