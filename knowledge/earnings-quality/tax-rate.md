# Effective tax rate — reading the gap to the statutory rate

## What it measures
The effective tax rate (ETR) is total tax expense — current plus deferred — divided by profit before tax. It should
sit near the statutory rate unless something specific moves it.

For Indian domestic companies the reference points are:
- About 25.17% (22% base rate plus surcharge and cess) for companies that opt for section 115BAA and give up most
  exemptions and incentives.
- About 34.94% (30% plus surcharge and cess) for companies with income above ₹100 crore that remain in the old
  regime; lower surcharge applies at lower income levels.
- Minimum Alternate Tax under section 115JB applies to companies in the old regime whose book profit tax exceeds
  normal tax; MAT paid can be carried forward as a credit.

## How to read it
- Reconcile the ETR using the tax reconciliation note required by Ind AS 12, which lists each reason the rate
  differs from the statutory rate.
- A consolidated ETR mixes subsidiaries in different jurisdictions and regimes; overseas profits may be taxed at
  lower or higher rates.
- Deferred tax movements often explain volatility: recognising a deferred tax asset on past losses lowers the rate,
  while writing one off raises it.
- A company transitioning to section 115BAA remeasures deferred tax balances, which produces a one-time swing.

## Red flags
- ETR persistently below 15% without a clear, disclosed incentive or loss carry-forward.
- Deferred tax assets recognised on accumulated losses in a company without a track record of taxable profits.
- Large swings in the quarterly tax rate that flatter net profit growth.
- High reported profit combined with very low current tax paid in the cash flow statement.
- ETR above 40%, which can indicate non-deductible expenses, losses in subsidiaries that cannot be offset, or
  profits that the tax authorities do not recognise.

## Benign explanations
- Tax holidays for units in Special Economic Zones, or legacy area-based incentives that are still running off.
- Use of brought-forward losses or unabsorbed depreciation.
- Transition to the new regime and the related one-time deferred tax remeasurement.
- Share of profit of associates and joint ventures, presented net of their tax, which lowers the apparent rate on
  consolidated PBT.

## Where to find it in Indian filings
- Income tax note with the effective tax rate reconciliation (Ind AS 12) in annual reports and restated financial
  information.
- Quarterly results show current and deferred tax for each period.
- Contingent liabilities note for disputed tax demands, and the "Outstanding Litigation" section of offer documents
  for tax proceedings.

## Questions for management
- Which tax regime does the company and each material subsidiary follow?
- What explains the difference between the effective and statutory rate, and how long will it persist?
- What is the basis for recognising deferred tax assets on losses?
