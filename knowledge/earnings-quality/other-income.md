# Other income — separating the core business from treasury and one-offs

## What it measures
Schedule III requires revenue from operations and other income to be shown separately. Other income typically
contains interest on deposits and loans, dividends, fair-value gains on investments, profit on sale of assets,
foreign exchange gains, rental income, and liabilities or provisions no longer required that are written back.
The share of profit before tax (PBT) that comes from other income shows how dependent headline profit is on items
outside the operating business.

## How to read it
- Start with EBITDA or operating EBIT, which exclude other income, and compare its trend with PBT. If PBT grows
  while operating profit is flat or falling, the growth is coming from other income.
- Break other income into recurring (interest on a stable cash pile, rental income) and non-recurring (gains on
  sale, write-backs, one-time foreign exchange gains).
- Relate interest income to the average cash and investments it is earned on; a yield far above market deposit
  rates suggests loans to parties that deserve scrutiny.
- In quarterly results, a sudden jump in other income against the same quarter last year deserves an explanation
  in the results commentary.

## Red flags
- Other income above roughly a third of PBT in a non-financial company, particularly while operating margins fall.
- Large "liabilities no longer required written back" or "provisions written back" in other income.
- Interest income on inter-corporate deposits or loans to promoter group entities.
- Fair-value gains on unlisted investments, which rely on valuation assumptions.
- Foreign exchange gains that are classified in other income while losses in earlier years were in expenses.

## Benign explanations
- Cash-rich companies (common in IT services and some consumer companies) earning interest on genuine surplus cash
  held in bank deposits or liquid mutual funds.
- Proceeds of a recent equity raise temporarily invested in deposits before deployment for the stated objects.
- Export incentives or government grants that are recurring and linked to operations, sometimes presented as other
  operating revenue instead.

## Where to find it in Indian filings
- "Other income" note to the statement of profit and loss, which Schedule III requires to be disaggregated into
  interest income, dividend income and other non-operating income.
- Quarterly results under Regulation 33 of the LODR Regulations show other income as a separate line for the
  quarter, previous quarter, same quarter last year and year to date.
- Related party transactions note for interest earned from related parties.

## Questions for management
- What are the components of other income this period, and which of them do you expect to recur?
- On what balances is interest income earned, and are any of those balances with related parties?
- Excluding other income and exceptional items, how did operating profit move?
