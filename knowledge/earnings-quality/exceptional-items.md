# Exceptional items — when "one-off" is not one-off

## What it measures
Schedule III provides a line for exceptional items between operating profit and profit before tax. Companies use
it for items whose size or nature makes separate presentation useful: impairments, restructuring and voluntary
retirement costs, gains or losses on sale of businesses, litigation settlements, and the impact of one-time
regulatory changes. Because the line sits outside "ordinary" profit, it is also an obvious place to put costs a
company would prefer investors to ignore.

## How to read it
- Compute growth and margins both including and excluding exceptional items, and present both.
- Check the pattern across years: charges that appear every year are part of the cost of running the business.
- Gains deserve as much attention as losses. A gain on sale of a property or subsidiary can turn a weak year into
  a record one.
- Distinguish discontinued operations (Ind AS 105), which are presented separately after tax, from exceptional
  items within continuing operations.

## Red flags
- Exceptional charges in three or more consecutive years.
- Exceptional gains that account for more than a quarter of PBT, especially when the results commentary highlights
  headline profit growth.
- Restructuring costs recurring alongside falling operating margins, suggesting the restructuring is not working.
- Items moved into or out of the exceptional line compared with the prior-year presentation.

## Benign explanations
- A genuinely non-recurring event with clear disclosure: a single plant closure, a court-ordered settlement, the
  sale of a non-core business.
- One-time effects of a regulatory change, such as the remeasurement of deferred tax or employee benefits when a
  new code or rate takes effect.

## Where to find it in Indian filings
- Face of the statement of profit and loss and the quarterly results format under Regulation 33, with a note
  describing each item.
- Notes to accounts and the directors' report for the nature of each exceptional item.
- In offer documents, the restated financial information and the MD&A section explaining significant items.

## Questions for management
- What is included in exceptional items and why is each considered exceptional?
- Have similar items appeared in earlier periods?
- What would profit growth have been excluding exceptional items in both periods?
