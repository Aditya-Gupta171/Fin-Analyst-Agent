# Reading an Indian annual report

## What it measures
The annual report of a listed Indian company contains the audited standalone and consolidated financial statements
together with several statutory reports. The notes and the reports around the statements often matter more than
the statements themselves.

## How to read it
- **Independent auditor's reports** (standalone and consolidated) with key audit matters, the CARO 2020 annexure and
  the report on internal financial controls. Read these first.
- **Notes to accounts** — significant accounting policies, judgements and estimates, related parties, contingent
  liabilities, segment information, financial risk management, and Schedule III additional regulatory information
  (ageing schedules, ratios with explanations for changes above 25%, undisclosed income, benami property, wilful
  defaulter status).
- **Directors' report** — dividends, transfers to reserves, material changes after year end, related party contracts
  (Form AOC-2), subsidiaries' performance (Form AOC-1), employee remuneration ratios.
- **Management Discussion and Analysis** — industry, segment performance, key ratio changes and their reasons.
- **Corporate governance report** — board composition, audit committee meetings and attendance, auditor fees,
  pending investor complaints, related party policy.
- **Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report** — mandatory for the largest listed companies.
- **Notice of the annual general meeting** — auditor appointments, remuneration and related party approvals.

## Red flags
- Explanations in the ratio disclosures that are generic or missing for large changes.
- Subsidiaries with losses or negative net worth funded by the parent (Form AOC-1).
- Low attendance or frequent resignations of independent directors.
- Material events after the balance sheet date disclosed only in the directors' report.

## Benign explanations
- One-time changes that are well explained and consistent with the prior year's commentary.

## Where to find it in Indian filings
- Company investor relations pages and stock exchange filings under LODR Regulation 34, which requires the annual
  report to be submitted to the exchanges.

## Questions for management
- What explains the largest year-on-year changes in the Schedule III ratios?
- How are loss-making subsidiaries being funded, and what is the plan for them?
