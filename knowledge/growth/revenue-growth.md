# Revenue growth — quality, sources and comparisons

## What it measures
Revenue growth compares revenue from operations with a prior period: year on year (the same period last year,
which removes seasonality) or sequentially (the immediately preceding quarter, which shows momentum).

## How to read it
- Decompose growth into volume, price, mix, currency and acquisitions. Price-led growth in an inflationary year is
  lower quality than volume-led growth.
- Organic growth excludes acquisitions; inorganic growth can hide a shrinking core business.
- Check revenue recognition: gross versus net presentation (principal versus agent under Ind AS 115) can inflate
  reported revenue without changing economics, which is common in trading, travel and e-commerce.
- Compare growth with receivables and cash collection. Growth that shows up as receivables rather than cash is not
  yet proven.
- Sequential declines are normal in seasonal businesses; year-on-year declines of more than about 10% are not.

## Red flags
- Revenue growth concentrated in the last quarter of the year, followed by a weak first quarter.
- Growth coming from related parties or a few new customers.
- A change in revenue recognition policy or in gross versus net presentation in the year growth accelerated.
- Year-on-year revenue decline in a quarter without an industry-wide explanation.

## Benign explanations
- Seasonality, festive timing (Diwali shifting between quarters), monsoon effects on rural demand.
- A large contract ending as planned, disclosed in advance.
- Commodity price declines lowering realisations while volumes grow.

## Where to find it in Indian filings
- Revenue from contracts with customers note: disaggregation by geography, product and timing of recognition.
- Segment reporting under Ind AS 108.
- Quarterly results under LODR Regulation 33: current quarter, previous quarter, same quarter last year and year to
  date; results must be filed within 45 days of quarter end (60 days for the annual audited results).

## Questions for management
- How much of the growth came from volume, price, mix and acquisitions?
- What share of revenue comes from new customers and from related parties?
