# Profit and EPS growth — what is really driving earnings

## What it measures
Growth in EBITDA, profit after tax and diluted EPS against the comparable prior period. Together they show whether
earnings growth comes from the operating business and whether shareholders benefit per share.

## How to read it
- Build a profit bridge: change in EBITDA, less change in depreciation and finance costs, plus change in other
  income and exceptional items, less change in tax.
- Profit growth well ahead of EBITDA growth points to non-operating drivers: lower tax, higher other income or
  exceptional gains.
- Growth off a low or loss-making base produces very large percentages; look at absolute amounts.
- EPS growth below profit growth indicates dilution; above it, a buyback.

## Red flags
- Net profit growth driven mainly by other income, exceptional gains or a lower tax rate.
- EBITDA declining while net profit rises.
- EPS growth far below profit growth after large preferential allotments.

## Benign explanations
- Deleveraging that lowers finance costs, visible in borrowings.
- Utilisation of tax losses with a clear horizon.

## Where to find it in Indian filings
- Statement of profit and loss and quarterly results; EPS note.
- MD&A explanations of changes in expenses and profit.

## Questions for management
- Excluding other income, exceptional items and tax changes, how did profit grow?
- How did share count change during the period?
