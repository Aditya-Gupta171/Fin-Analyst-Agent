# The auditor's report — opinions, emphasis of matter, KAMs and auditor changes

## What it measures
The independent auditor's report states whether the financial statements give a true and fair view. Indian
auditing standards (issued by ICAI, aligned with ISA) define its parts:
- **Opinion** (SA 700 / SA 705): unmodified, qualified ("except for"), adverse (the statements are materially
  misstated) or disclaimer (the auditor could not obtain sufficient evidence to form an opinion).
- **Emphasis of matter** (SA 706): draws attention to a matter already disclosed that is fundamental to users'
  understanding, without modifying the opinion.
- **Material uncertainty related to going concern** (SA 570).
- **Key audit matters** (SA 701): the matters of most significance in the audit, required for listed entities.
- **CARO 2020**: the Companies (Auditor's Report) Order requires additional reporting on matters such as loans to
  related parties, defaults in repayment, use of short-term funds for long-term purposes, quarterly returns filed
  with banks, and whistle-blower complaints.

## How to read it
- Any modified opinion is serious; read the "basis for opinion" paragraph and quantify the effect on the metrics
  being analysed.
- An emphasis of matter on going concern, a regulatory investigation or a large unresolved claim changes how every
  other number should be read.
- Key audit matters point to the areas of greatest judgement — revenue recognition, impairment, provisions,
  capitalisation. Cross-check them against the red flags from the numbers.
- Read the auditor's report on internal financial controls over financial reporting, required under the Companies
  Act; a qualified report means controls are not reliable.
- For consolidated statements, note components audited by other auditors and any qualification in subsidiaries.

## Red flags
- Qualified, adverse or disclaimer opinion, or a material uncertainty on going concern.
- A mid-term resignation of the statutory auditor rather than rotation at the end of its term. Section 139 of the
  Companies Act limits an audit firm to two terms of five years; resignations outside that pattern, especially
  close to results, are a well-known warning sign.
- CARO remarks on defaults, discrepancies between stock statements submitted to banks and the books, or funds
  raised for short-term purposes used for long-term purposes.
- Qualified report on internal financial controls.
- Key audit matters on revenue recognition in a company with weak cash conversion.

## Benign explanations
- Mandatory rotation at the end of an auditor's term, disclosed in the notice of the annual general meeting.
- Emphasis of matter on a disclosed, industry-wide event (for example, a regulatory change affecting all
  participants) rather than company-specific uncertainty.

## Where to find it in Indian filings
- Independent auditor's report on standalone and consolidated financial statements in the annual report, with the
  CARO annexure and the report on internal financial controls.
- For quarterly results, the statutory auditor's limited review report, which can also be modified.
- Stock exchange disclosures of auditor resignation: SEBI requires the listed company to disclose the resignation
  and the detailed reasons provided by the auditor, and the outgoing auditor files reasons with the Registrar under
  section 140.
- Offer documents: examination report on restated financial information and any qualifications in past audit
  reports, which ICDR requires to be disclosed.

## Questions for management
- What is the basis of the modification or emphasis of matter, and what is its financial impact?
- Why did the auditor resign, and did the outgoing auditor raise any concerns?
- What steps have been taken to address matters reported under CARO or on internal financial controls?
