# Related party transactions and value leakage

## What it measures
Related parties under Ind AS 24 include promoters, key managerial personnel, their relatives, and entities they
control or significantly influence. Transactions with them are not wrong in themselves, but they may not be on
arm's-length terms and are the most common route for moving value from minority shareholders to controlling ones.

## How to read it
- Measure scale: related-party revenue and purchases as a share of the company's totals; loans, advances and
  guarantees relative to net worth.
- Look at direction of cash: sales to related parties with long receivable periods, purchases at high prices,
  loans at low interest, royalties or brand fees paid to promoter entities.
- Approval regime: under SEBI LODR Regulation 23, all related party transactions need audit committee approval and
  material ones need shareholder approval, with related parties not voting in favour. A transaction is material if,
  alone or with prior transactions in the year, it exceeds ₹1,000 crore or 10% of annual consolidated turnover,
  whichever is lower.
- Check for entities that are economically related but not formally disclosed as related parties (common
  directors, shared addresses, recently incorporated suppliers).

## Red flags
- More than about a fifth of revenue from related parties, or rising sharply.
- Loans and advances to promoter group entities above about a tenth of net worth, especially interest-free or
  repeatedly rolled over.
- Royalty or brand fees to promoter entities growing faster than revenue.
- Purchase of assets from or sale of assets to related parties at values not supported by independent valuation.
- CARO remarks on loans or guarantees to related parties that are prejudicial to the company's interest.

## Benign explanations
- Group structures where a sister company is a captive supplier or distributor, with long, consistent history and
  pricing benchmarked to third parties.
- Transactions with wholly owned subsidiaries, which eliminate on consolidation.

## Where to find it in Indian filings
- Related party disclosures note (Ind AS 24) in standalone and consolidated statements.
- Form AOC-2 in the directors' report for contracts under section 188 of the Companies Act.
- Half-yearly related party transaction disclosures filed with stock exchanges under LODR.
- Offer documents: "Related Party Transactions" in restated financial information and a summary in the offer
  document summary section.

## Questions for management
- Are related-party sales and purchases priced at arm's length, and how is that established?
- What loans, advances and guarantees are outstanding with related parties, and on what terms?
- Were material related party transactions approved by shareholders under Regulation 23?
