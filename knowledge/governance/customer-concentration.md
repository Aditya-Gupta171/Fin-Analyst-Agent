# Customer and supplier concentration

## What it measures
The share of revenue from the largest customer and the top ten customers; similarly for suppliers. Ind AS 108
requires disclosure when a single external customer accounts for 10% or more of revenue.

## How to read it
- Concentration increases the impact of losing, or renegotiating with, a single customer.
- Concentrated customers usually hold pricing power, which caps margins and stretches payment terms.
- Judge concentration with contract tenure, switching costs and the customer's credit quality. A long-term
  contract with a large, creditworthy buyer is less risky than repeated short orders.
- Combine with receivables: concentration plus rising DSO is a particularly weak position.

## Red flags
- Largest customer above about a quarter of revenue, or top ten above about 60%.
- Concentration increasing as the company approaches an IPO.
- Key customers that are related parties or that are also suppliers.
- Contracts up for renewal shortly after listing.

## Benign explanations
- Industries with structurally few buyers (automotive component suppliers to a few OEMs, defence suppliers to the
  government, contract manufacturers for a few brands) where relationships are long-standing.

## Where to find it in Indian filings
- Segment note (Ind AS 108) for customers above 10% of revenue.
- Offer documents: "Risk Factors" and "Our Business" usually disclose revenue from top customers and suppliers,
  often anonymised, across the restated periods.

## Questions for management
- What share of revenue comes from the top customer and top ten, and how has it changed?
- What are the contract terms and renewal dates for the largest customers?
