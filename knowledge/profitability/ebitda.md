# EBITDA and operating profit — definitions and adjustments

## What it measures
EBITDA — earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation — approximates operating profit before the
cost of assets and financing. From a Schedule III statement it is revenue from operations less total expenses,
adding back finance costs and depreciation and amortisation. This engine excludes other income, so EBITDA reflects
the operating business only. Operating EBIT deducts depreciation from EBITDA.

## How to read it
- EBITDA is not defined in Ind AS; companies choose their own definitions. Check whether other income, exceptional
  items, ESOP costs or foreign exchange differences are included.
- Ind AS 116 moved lease rentals out of operating expenses into depreciation and interest, raising EBITDA for
  lease-heavy businesses without any economic change. Compare pre-Ind AS 116 or rent-adjusted figures for retail,
  hospitals, airlines and restaurants.
- "Adjusted EBITDA" that excludes share-based payments or recurring restructuring costs overstates profitability,
  common in consumer internet companies.
- EBITDA ignores working capital and capex, so it must be read alongside operating and free cash flow.

## Red flags
- Company-defined EBITDA materially above EBITDA computed from the statement of profit and loss.
- Growing gap between EBITDA and operating cash flow.
- Recurring costs excluded as "one-time" in adjusted EBITDA.

## Benign explanations
- Consistent, clearly reconciled adjustments that remove genuinely non-cash or non-recurring items.

## Where to find it in Indian filings
- Statement of profit and loss (compute directly) and the company's own EBITDA disclosure in results
  presentations or MD&A.
- In offer documents, EBITDA is presented as a non-GAAP key performance indicator with a reconciliation, and KPIs
  disclosed in the "Basis for Offer Price" must be certified.

## Questions for management
- How is EBITDA defined, and how does it reconcile to the statement of profit and loss?
- What is EBITDA after lease payments?
