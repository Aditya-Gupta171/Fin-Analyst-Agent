# Margins, margin compression and losses

## What it measures
Margins express profit as a share of revenue at different levels: gross margin after direct material costs,
EBITDA margin after all operating costs, and net profit margin after depreciation, interest, tax and non-operating
items. Their trend reveals pricing power, cost control and operating leverage.

## How to read it
- Attribute changes to causes: input costs (commodity prices, currency), pricing, product or customer mix,
  utilisation of fixed costs, and one-offs.
- Gross margin is only meaningful for businesses with material costs; for IT services, employee cost is the
  relevant direct cost.
- EBITDA margin compression of more than about 400 basis points in a year, or 500 basis points quarter on quarter,
  needs a specific explanation.
- A net margin moving differently from EBITDA margin points to depreciation, interest, other income, exceptional
  items or tax.
- For loss-making companies, look at the trajectory of contribution margin and EBITDA, the cash runway, and
  whether losses are narrowing as revenue scales.

## Red flags
- Margin compression combined with rising receivables or inventory — the company may be buying revenue.
- Margins far above listed peers without an explanation, particularly in an offer document.
- Swing from profit to loss against the same quarter last year.
- Losses in every year of an offer document's track record with no narrowing trend.

## Benign explanations
- Commodity input price spikes that are passed through with a lag, visible in industry data.
- Deliberate investment in growth (marketing, new stores, hiring ahead of demand) with disclosed intent.
- Seasonal quarters with lower fixed-cost absorption.

## Where to find it in Indian filings
- Statement of profit and loss and quarterly results; segment results for business-level margins.
- MD&A explanations of changes in expenses and margins.
- Schedule III additional regulatory information: net profit ratio with explanation for changes above 25%.
- Offer documents list issuers that do not meet ICDR Regulation 6(1) profitability requirements; they can list
  under Regulation 6(2), which requires at least 75% of the offer to be allocated to qualified institutional
  buyers.

## Questions for management
- What caused the change in margins, and how much of it is expected to reverse?
- What price increases have been taken and when do they take effect?
- For loss-making businesses: what is the path to breakeven and the funding runway?
