# Cash conversion cycle and trade working capital

## What it measures
The cash conversion cycle (CCC) is DSO plus DIO minus DPO: the number of days between paying for inputs and
collecting cash from customers. Trade working capital — receivables plus inventory minus payables — is the amount
of money tied up in that cycle. Growth consumes cash in proportion to working capital intensity: a company with a
90-day cycle must finance roughly a quarter of its incremental annual revenue.

## How to read it
- Negative cycles are common in consumer staples, retail and platform businesses that collect before they pay;
  they generate cash as they grow.
- A lengthening cycle increases funding needs and usually shows up as higher short-term borrowings.
- Break any change into its three components; each has different explanations and risks.
- Compare trade working capital as a share of revenue over several years to separate trend from timing noise.

## Red flags
- Cycle lengthening in consecutive years while revenue growth slows.
- Working capital growth funded by short-term borrowings or commercial paper.
- Improvement driven entirely by stretching payables while receivable and inventory days worsen.

## Benign explanations
- Business mix shifting towards segments with structurally longer cycles, disclosed in segment reporting.
- One-time inventory or receivable build around a disruption, reversing in the following quarters.

## Where to find it in Indian filings
- Trade receivables, inventories and trade payables notes, and the working-capital adjustments in the cash flow
  statement.
- Schedule III additional regulatory information requires ratios including trade receivables turnover, inventory
  turnover and trade payables turnover, with explanations for changes above 25%.
- Offer documents: MD&A and the "Objects of the Offer" when proceeds are to fund working capital, which must be
  supported by a working capital assessment.

## Questions for management
- Which component drove the change in the cycle?
- How is incremental working capital being funded?
