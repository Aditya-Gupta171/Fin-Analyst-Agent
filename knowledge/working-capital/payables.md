# Trade payables — supplier financing and liquidity stress

## What it measures
Trade payables are amounts owed to suppliers. Days payables outstanding (DPO) — average payables divided by cost of
goods sold, times days — shows how long the company takes to pay. Suppliers extending credit is a legitimate,
interest-free source of working capital; a sharp lengthening of the payment period can also be a symptom of cash
stress.

## How to read it
- A rising DPO lowers the cash conversion cycle and improves operating cash flow in that year, but the benefit
  does not repeat and reverses if suppliers push back.
- Check whether operating cash flow improved mainly because payables grew; if so, cash conversion is weaker than it
  looks.
- Supply-chain finance or reverse factoring arrangements can keep amounts owed to banks inside trade payables.
  Understand whether such programmes exist and how they are presented.
- Indian rules limit how far micro and small suppliers can be stretched: the MSMED Act requires payment within the
  agreed period and no later than 45 days, and since FY24 section 43B(h) of the Income-tax Act disallows the
  deduction for amounts owed to micro and small enterprises that remain unpaid beyond that limit.

## Red flags
- DPO lengthening by more than about a month in a year while operating cash flow "improves".
- Growing amounts overdue to micro and small enterprises, or interest payable to them under the MSMED Act.
- Payables classified as "acceptances" or supplier credit rising quickly — these can be bank-funded.
- Suppliers demanding advances or security deposits, disclosed in MD&A or risk factors.

## Benign explanations
- Negotiated longer terms reflecting increased purchasing scale.
- A shift in supplier mix towards large vendors with standard longer terms.
- Timing of large capital or raw material purchases around the year end.

## Where to find it in Indian filings
- Trade payables note, including the Schedule III ageing schedule split between MSME and others, and disputed dues.
- Disclosure of amounts due to micro and small enterprises and interest thereon required under the MSMED Act.
- Cash flow statement working-capital adjustments.

## Questions for management
- Why did the payment period lengthen, and is it sustainable with suppliers?
- Does the company use supplier finance, bill acceptance or reverse factoring programmes?
- Are there overdue amounts to micro and small suppliers?
