# Inventory build-up, obsolescence and margin flattering

## What it measures
Inventory covers raw materials, work in progress, finished goods, stock-in-trade and stores and spares. Days
inventory outstanding (DIO) — average inventory divided by cost of goods sold, times days — shows how long stock
sits before it is sold. Comparing inventory growth with revenue growth shows whether stock is accumulating ahead
of demand.

## How to read it
- Look at the composition. A build-up in raw materials ahead of an expected price increase is a very different
  signal from a build-up in finished goods.
- Under Ind AS 2, production overheads are absorbed into the cost of inventory. Producing more than is sold moves
  fixed costs from the income statement into the balance sheet and temporarily raises margins.
- Inventory is carried at the lower of cost and net realisable value; slow-moving stock should be written down.
  A growing stock of finished goods without write-downs deserves scrutiny.
- In real estate, inventory is land and construction work in progress and can span many years; judge it with
  project-level data rather than DIO.

## Red flags
- Inventory growing more than about 25 percentage points faster than revenue, concentrated in finished goods.
- Gross margin improving while inventory days rise — consistent with overhead absorption rather than pricing power.
- Falling inventory write-downs while sales slow.
- Inventory funded by short-term borrowings that are rising faster than revenue.

## Benign explanations
- Pre-season stocking in seasonal businesses (fertilisers, agrochemicals, air conditioners) when the year end
  precedes the season.
- Strategic raw material purchases ahead of disclosed price increases or supply disruptions.
- New plants or product launches requiring initial stock.
- Commodity price increases raising the value of the same physical quantity.

## Where to find it in Indian filings
- Inventories note with the break-up by category and the valuation policy.
- "Changes in inventories of finished goods, work-in-progress and stock-in-trade" line in the statement of profit
  and loss.
- Quarterly results: the same line helps detect production running ahead of sales within the year.

## Questions for management
- Which categories of inventory increased and why?
- What is the ageing of finished goods, and what write-downs were recognised?
- Was production during the period higher than sales, and what was the effect on margins?
